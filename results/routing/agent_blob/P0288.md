# Scene prompt
Create a compact 3D open-world city map for a Police vs. Robber game, sized for 16 players and quick vehicle-based crossings between districts. A central financial downtown contains skyscrapers, a bank vault, jewelry stores, narrow alleys, and tight grid streets; connect it to an industrial docks district with warehouses, shipping-container yards, water access, armored transport routes, and laundering locations. Include a suburban residential zone with open roads, garages, apartments or hidden safehouses, and convenience stores, plus a high-security prison complex on the city edge. Keep heist interiors seamlessly connected to the street-level map, with continuous entrances and escape routes through alleys, subway tunnels, sewer networks, and roads. Arrange broad pursuit routes between districts with traffic intersections, drawbridges, and roadblock points, while helicopters and boats have clear access around the city.

# Agent decision

## Genre
Simulation is dominant because Police and Robber roles operate repeatable patrol, heist, pursuit, and incarceration tasks in a persistent shared city. Shooter is secondary because on-foot engagements depend on cover, approaches, and containment.

## Shape and preset
Use `world-open`, a single shared city traversed freely by both teams. The preset is Role Sim: retain its task stations, circulation, and vehicle-road network, specialized here for asymmetric police and robbery work.

## Config requirements
Use `trigger-task` for physically visible heist, laundering, police, and prison task sites; `path-circulation` for continuous movement among districts; and `path-road-vehicle` for broad pursuit roads and armored transport routes. Add `cover-los` for alleys, container yards, and urban cover; `path-flank-tunnel` for subway and sewer escapes; `building-interior` for seamless bank, jewelry-store, warehouse, apartment, safehouse, convenience-store, and prison interiors; `settlement-density` for the compact city fabric; and `water-body` for navigable docks water. Roadblocks and drawbridges remain explicit scene geometry.

## Layout requirements
For `trigger-task`, place usable task markers at every authored heist target, laundering location, police facility, and prison interaction site, without inventing extra sites. Connect each to nearby roads and at least one escape approach. The 16-player target governs capacity rather than creating sixteen markers. No additional invisible pickups or spawners are required by this scene.

## Layout components
Downtown sits centrally with tight grid streets, skyscrapers, bank vault, jewelry stores, and alleys. Industrial docks occupy one side, containing warehouses, container yards, waterfront, armored routes, drawbridges, and laundering sites. Suburbs spread opposite with wider roads, garages, apartments or safehouses, and convenience stores. The prison forms a secure edge compound. Broad roads loop through all districts for quick vehicle crossings, while alleys, continuous interiors, subway tunnels, and sewers provide layered escapes. Waterways admit boats and open corridors admit helicopters. Conceal the compact outer boundary behind dense development, water, and prison-edge infrastructure.

## Render order
Render `topdown` first and treat it as authoritative, because disconnected pursuit loops, blocked district links, or escape routes without exits would break the asymmetric play.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
Scale is large but compact, sized for 16 players and only a few blocks per district. The theme is contemporary urban crime sandbox. Use `P2` for tunnels beneath streets, `P3` for seamless exterior-to-interior play, and `CHECK` for navigable water and helicopter airspace.
