## Enriched image prompt
Create three separate, small open-air migration legs carrying a duck from a New Zealand departure coast toward a Canadian destination coast. Each leg crosses rough dark sea beneath naturalistic threatening weather, with a readable airborne route weaving past storm cells and enough open air to continue forward; the first leg is a compact playable ocean airspace. Use the departure shoreline only in the first map, open ocean in the middle map, and the destination shoreline only in the final map rather than placing both countries on one tiny surface.

## Genre
The dominant genre is Adventure because the core is a directed migration journey through environmental danger.

## Shape and preset
Use `world-chaptered`: the few migration legs are genuinely separate maps rather than one world-scale ocean surface. Preset is none, because no Adventure preset expresses a staged airborne migration without importing unrelated story gates or ground trails.

## Config requirements
`hazard-kill` draws dangerous storm cells that constrain each flight corridor. Universal `water-body` draws the rough dark sea beneath every leg. Reach outside Adventure's shortlist to Racing's `volume-open` for the traversable open-air migration volume, and use `landmark-focal` for the next safe horizon or final Canadian coastline that pulls the duck forward.

## Layout requirements
Place one entry SpawnZone and one forward stage-exit marker in each of the three flight legs, at opposite ends of its traversable airspace. Place `checkpoint-respawn` once at the completed end of each non-final leg, for two total, so failure returns the duck to the latest migration boundary.

## Layout components
The Departure Leg is a separate map with a New Zealand shoreline behind its `volume-open-air` route; the Open-Ocean Storm Leg is a separate map with rough sea on every side; and the Arrival Leg is a separate map whose `volume-open-air` route ends at a Canadian shoreline. Each bounded ocean airspace has threatening cloud and storm regions around the route, an entry side, and an opposite stage boundary. Keep one clear continuous flyable volume between hazards in each map and frame the next horizon as the direction of travel.

## Render order
Use `topdown`: every leg needs a continuous route through the storm regions before the naturalistic weather and ocean depth are dressed.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
The scale band is small per flight leg, following the author's request for a playable small initial map. The theme is naturalistic, stormy ocean migration. `P4` is required because the legs are separate maps, and `CHECK` is required because flight uses a volumetric airspace whose hazards must not self-occlude the route.
