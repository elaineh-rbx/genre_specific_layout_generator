# Scene prompt
Create a small, bright toon-style 3D garden game map with a clear, cheerful layout for collecting berries. Arrange berry-bearing plants around the garden with open paths and unobstructed space between them.

# Agent decision
## Genre
The dominant genre is Adventure: the spatial activity is walking through a garden to find and collect berries. The score is gameplay state, not a simulation economy or a second layout genre.

## Shape and preset
Use `space-bounded`, taken from outside Adventure's usual shapes, because the entire activity fits inside one clearly bounded, single-level garden rather than an open world. The preset is Scavenger Hunt, retaining collectible objectives while substituting the compact garden shape and omitting hidden alcoves and a quest board that were not requested.

## Config requirements
Use `path-trail` for open garden paths that circulate between planting areas. Draw clusters of berry-bearing plants around those paths with clear, unobstructed floor between clusters; the plants are required scene geometry but have no separate canonical option. Keep all planting beds at ground level and avoid dense decorative clutter that could conceal or block pickups.

## Layout requirements
Place `collectible-nodes` as berries on or immediately beside the berry-bearing plants, distributed across the full garden rather than concentrated at one bed. No berry count was stated, so do not invent one. Every pickup must be reachable from an adjacent path or clear standing patch, with enough clearance that plant collision does not obstruct collection.

## Layout components
Build one small, enclosed garden zone on a flat surface. Arrange several planting beds and berry-bush clusters around a simple loop of broad paths, leaving open crossings and clear space around every plant group. Use a cheerful central planting feature or open lawn to organize circulation without adding a separate gameplay zone. Keep sightlines short and readable, with low garden props and no interiors, overhangs, or hidden lower layers. Enclose the perimeter with a bright toon fence or hedge. Compose the frame as a compact garden plan whose path loop, planting clusters, and collectible access spaces are immediately legible.

## Render order
Use `isometric`: establish the bright toon garden and plant arrangement first, then derive the top-down plan, because the simple loop does not require computed topology.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
The scale is small, directly matching the requested compact garden. The theme is bright, clear, cheerful toon horticulture. Use `P0`; everything occupies one flat, bounded outdoor surface with no geometry forcing another capture pass.
