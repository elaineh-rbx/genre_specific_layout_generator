# Scene prompt

Create a 3D game map with a soft rustic lobby and a separate small hotel round area. The lobby has dim lamps and elegant sit-down seating arranged as a relaxed waiting space. The hotel shares the same rustic aesthetic and is one connected, shared interior: a handful of rooms linked by hallways, compact enough for players to sweep through within three minutes, with open circulation between rooms so the group can observe one another without isolation.

# Agent decision

## Genre

The dominant genre is Party & Casual: this is a social, round-based guessing game staged for a group.

## Shape and preset

Use `space-staged` from the Minigame preset. The relaxed lobby and the hotel round area are genuinely separate maps, with the hotel hidden from waiting players until the round begins. Keep the preset’s transport concept but omit its leaderboard wall, spectator area, and physical scoring volume because the brief does not require them.

## Config requirements

Draw `social-hub` as the soft rustic lobby, comfortably sized for all ten possible players, with dim lamps and clusters of elegant usable seats. Draw both ends of `teleporter-link`: an unobtrusive departure point in the lobby and an arrival setting inside the hotel. The hotel is a compact shared interior rather than an outside-to-inside transition.

## Layout requirements

Place `teleporter-link` markers at the lobby departure area and hotel arrival area, returning players to the lobby after each round. Place up to ten `spawn-protected` round starts across the hotel’s connected circulation space so entrants are distributed fairly without being isolated in closed rooms.

## Layout components

Build two zones: a warm waiting lobby with lamp-lit seating groups, and a separate small hotel interior. The hotel contains a handful of rustic rooms linked by short hallways and broad open doorways, forming one readable circulation network that can be swept in three minutes. Keep sightlines partial but preserve easy movement and observation between rooms. Enclose both spaces as finished interiors and frame each from a clear three-quarter overview.

## Render order

Use `isometric`: render each space’s isometric view first and treat it as authoritative, then derive its top-down plan, since exact computed topology is unnecessary.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost

The scale is small, driven by the three-minute sweep and handful of rooms. The theme is soft rustic hotel elegance with warm dim lighting. Use `P4` because lobby and round hotel are separate maps.
