# Agent decision

## Clarifications resolved
- [author] shape — Q: Does each player build their own fortress, or does the whole server build one together? A: Each player builds their own fortress on a separate tycoon plot.
- [author] goal — Q: Who is the army for? Defending against attacking waves, fighting other players' fortresses, or just parading? A: The army is for defending the player's fortress against attacking waves.
- [author] scale — Q: Roughly how big should the army get? A dozen soldiers and a hundred are different builds. A: Keep it to roughly a dozen soldiers per player for a small, manageable first build.

## Enriched image prompt
Create a medieval fortress tycoon made of large, independent, evenly spaced player plots. Give each plot a clear buildable footprint with a fortress keep as its defensive heart, surrounding walls and gates that can visibly progress through construction tiers, internal walking space, and a small barracks or muster yard sized for roughly a dozen allied soldiers. Leave open defensive ground between each plot's outer wave-entry edge and the fortress so attacking enemies can approach dynamically while the player's army intercepts them. Keep neighboring plots far enough apart that fortresses, armies, and enemy waves never overlap.

## Genre
The dominant genre is Simulation, canonical slug `simulation`, because each player develops a personal tycoon fortress. Strategy, canonical slug `strategy`, is secondary because the army defends that fortress from attacking waves.

## Shape and preset
Use `plot-isolated`, BuildZone (Isolated Per-Player Plots), because every player builds on a separate non-overlapping tycoon plot. The matched preset is Tycoon, with its shared-team plot replaced by the confirmed per-player layout and its unrequested communal hub omitted.

## Config requirements
Use `gate-progression` for visible fortress construction thresholds and upgraded walls or gates. Use `path-circulation` for simple internal routes connecting the build area, keep, gate, and muster yard. Use the visible form of `tracker-core` for each central keep. The surrounding fortress walls, barracks, and roughly dozen-soldier muster area are scene-specific build content.

## Layout requirements
Place one `tracker-core` Tracker at the defended keep within each player plot. Place one `spawner-npc` Spawner at the outer edge of each plot for hostile waves, facing open defensive ground rather than another player's plot. Place one free-text allied Spawner at the barracks or muster yard and reserve roughly 12 soldier positions per player inside the fortress boundary.

## Layout components
Each repeated plot contains one buildable footprint, one central keep, progressive walls and gate, simple internal paths, one barracks or muster yard, roughly a dozen allied soldier positions, one hostile wave origin, and open approach ground. Wide neutral gaps separate the plots. Compose each plot as a self-contained medieval fortress-defense unit that can repeat without overlap.

## Render order
Use `topdown`: plan one repeatable isolated plot, its defensive ground, keep, gate, wave origin, and muster capacity first, then dress it isometrically as a medieval fortress. The top-down plan is authoritative because repeated plots must remain independent and wave approach ground must not cross neighboring builds.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
The scale is medium per player plot, driven by one manageable fortress and roughly a dozen soldiers while retaining gates and paths as readable details. The overall grid may expand with player count without changing that unit. The theme is a grounded medieval fortress tycoon. Use `P0`; waves path dynamically across open ground, and no fixed procedural lane, enterable interior, overhang, or separate map is required.
