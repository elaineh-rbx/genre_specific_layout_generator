# Agent decision

## Clarifications resolved
- [author] scale — Q: How large is 'giant' - can a hider cross the map in the 3-minute round, or should it be big enough that they never see the far side? A: Make it a compact giant-scale map: a hider can cross it in roughly 1–2 minutes, with dense cover and vertical routes making escapes and sightlines unpredictable.
- [author] shape — Q: Do players wait in a separate lobby between rounds, or do rounds start in place in the same forest? A: Use a small separate lobby between rounds, then teleport everyone into the forest when the round begins.

## Enriched image prompt
Create a compact giant-scale hide-and-seek forest that takes roughly 1–2 minutes to cross, separated from a small between-round lobby. Fill the forest with dense, overgrown cover: giant mossy rocks, hollow fallen trees, tall grass, enormous furniture with space underneath, ruined stone structures, elevated stone platforms, and hidden tunnels that reconnect to the open ground. Arrange vertical escape routes and broken sightlines without making distant areas unreachable. Use a moody twilight sky, light ground fog, and glowing mushrooms or lanterns to mark concealed paths. Keep one pitch-black Monster-sized circulation route and hider-sized hiding routes readable through the clutter without adding physical representations of timers, radar, or low gravity.

## Genre
The dominant genre is Party & Casual because this is explicitly a round-based Monster Tag / Hide & Seek game. Survival is secondary because a pursuing threat shapes cover and escape routes.

## Shape and preset
Use `space-staged`: a small lobby and the forest match area are separate maps. Match the Childhood Game preset but substitute its usual continuous-space shape with the author-required isolated lobby and stage. Retain dense hiding cover and lobby gathering, while dropping `obstacle-maze` because unpredictable cover is not the same as a procedural maze.

## Config requirements
Use `social-hub` for the compact between-round lobby. Use `cover-los` for giant rocks, hollow logs, tall grass, ruined stone, and giant furniture that break sightlines. Use `path-flank-tunnel` from the shared catalogue for the hidden tunnels linking distant parts of the forest beneath the surface. Use `teleporter-link` for lobby-to-forest transport. Elevated platforms and glowing path markers are prompt-specific visible geometry.

## Layout requirements
Place the paired `teleporter-link` endpoints in the lobby and forest arrival zone. Use `spawn-protected` with one Monster start point separated from the Hider start group by dense cover and enough travel distance to prevent an immediate tag. Exact player count is unspecified, so size the Hider start area for a group without inventing a slot count. Fog, low gravity, Monster radar, tag conversion, timers, and round results are mechanics rather than fixed map placements.

## Layout components
There are two map segments: one small lobby and one bounded overgrown forest. The forest contains scattered giant cover clusters, ruined stone structures, elevated platforms, reconnecting hidden tunnels, glowing path markers, and open circulation between dense hiding areas. Ensure tunnels have at least two exits and that platforms connect back to ground routes. Giant furniture and tunnels create genuine overhead surfaces.

## Render order
Use `isometric`: giant-scale atmosphere and layered cover composition lead, while the forest remains an open pursuit space rather than a maze requiring a computed blueprint.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
Use medium scale, driven by a 1–2 minute crossing while individual hiding props and tunnel mouths remain legible. The theme is atmospheric overgrown fantasy ruin at twilight. Use `P4` for the separate lobby and forest maps and `P2` for tunnels and giant furniture that overhang playable space.
