# Scene prompt
Create a compact gritty sci-fi cyber-bunker map for a single player competing alongside AI humanoid bots in simple metallic suits. Begin in a lobby that feeds into Game 1: a long metallic hallway sized for a roughly 20–30 second run, with a pulsing laser-core computer at the far end and glowing grid lines spanning the corridor. Beyond it, build Game 2: a high-tech chasm crossed in successive pairs of neon steps, each pair arranged as two side-by-side routes with one appearing as a holographic illusion over the drop. Place hovering security drones around the bunker spaces as hostile surveillance elements. The route ends at the far side of the chasm.

# Agent decision

## Genre
Party & Casual is dominant because this is a compact sequence of elimination minigames with a lobby and competitors. Obby & Platformer is secondary because Gravity Fractures is a player-paced jumping and route-guessing challenge.

## Shape and preset
Use `rooms-sequence`, outside Party & Casual’s usual shapes: one continuous bunker progresses from lobby to laser hallway to chasm crossing in a fixed order. Preset is none; Minigame assumes isolated rotating stages and lobby transport, while this prompt explicitly joins both games into one terminating route.

## Config requirements
Use `social-hub` for a lobby sized for the player plus 30 AI bots. Use `path-track` for the ordered neon-step crossing, `obstacle-guess` for successive solid-versus-holographic pairs, and `hazard-kill` for the sweeping laser grid and bottomless chasm. The pulsing laser-core computer, glowing grid lines, metallic-suited competitors, and hovering security drones are explicit scene geometry.

## Layout requirements
Place 31 `spawn-protected` starts in the lobby—one player start and 30 bot starts—distributed without blocking its exit. Place one `trigger-scoring` completion volume on the safe platform at the far side of Gravity Fractures. Each neon pair must expose two candidate step positions in sequence, but the number of pairs is unspecified. Do not add checkpoints: elimination is intentional, and success requires surviving both games.

## Layout components
An enclosed metallic lobby opens directly into a straight cyber-bunker hallway whose length supports a 20–30 second correctly timed run. Glowing grid lines span the corridor, and a pulsing laser-core computer anchors its far end. Beyond it, a high-tech chasm contains a sequential chain of side-by-side neon step pairs, with one member of each pair visually holographic over the drop. A safe terminal platform closes the route. Hovering security drones occupy visible surveillance positions around the lobby, hallway, and chasm. Industrial walls and the chasm void form the boundary; compose the scene as a legible forward sequence.

## Render order
Render `topdown` first and treat it as authoritative, because every neon-step choice and the continuous start-to-finish route must be valid to play.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
Scale is medium, driven by capacity for 31 competitors and a short two-game run. The theme is gritty neon cyber-bunker. Use `P6` because the ordered chasm crossing must be valid by construction.
