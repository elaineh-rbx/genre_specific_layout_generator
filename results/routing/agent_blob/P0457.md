# Scene prompt
Create a 3D arcade tennis map centered on a single standard singles court, with clear boundary lines, service boxes, a central net, and open run-off space around all sides. Use a bright, empty backdrop with no stadium seating or surrounding scenery, keeping the court as the sole focal point. Compose the view from an elevated third-person broadcast angle behind one baseline, showing both player positions across the net and most of the full court.

The dominant and only genre is Sports, because singles tennis scoring and player-versus-player court play define the experience.

The `trigger-scoring` option appears visibly as the central net and the in-bounds court regions used to judge valid tennis shots.

## Layout requirements
Place the functional half of `trigger-bounds` as one precise out-detection perimeter aligned to the singles lines. Place two `startpoint-play` reset positions, one centered behind each baseline, plus ball serve origins aligned to the active server. Place `trigger-scoring` coordinate regions across both court halves to detect legal bounces, net contact, and shots landing outside the boundary.

## Layout components
Build one regulation-proportioned singles court as the sole zone, centered in a bright empty plane. It has two equal court halves divided by one taut central net, crisp singles sidelines, two baselines, center service line, and four service boxes. Leave broad, unobstructed run-off on every side. Use a clean hard-court surface and a minimal open boundary fading into the bright backdrop, with no seating, buildings, terrain, or decorative scenery. Frame the full court symmetrically from an elevated position behind one baseline.

## Render order
Use `topdown`: render the plan first and treat it as authoritative, because exact court symmetry, line placement, and trigger alignment are necessary for playable tennis; dress the elevated isometric view from that plan.

The scale is small, driven by one singles court and its run-off. The theme is bright, clean, arcade-sport minimalism. Route through `P0`; the bounded single-level surface introduces no overhang, interior transition, separate map, or volumetric traversal cost.
