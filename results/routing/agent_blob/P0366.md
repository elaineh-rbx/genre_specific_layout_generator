# Scene prompt

Create a 3D point-to-point racing map with one continuous route traveling through three distinct environments. The course begins in a city on normal paved roads, transitions into a desert section with loose sand terrain, and finishes in a snow-covered area with icy road surfaces; the endpoint is separate from the starting area.

# Agent decision

## Genre

The genre is Racing. The player controls forward speed along a finite course with a distinct endpoint, so this is not an infinite runner or a vehicle simulation.

## Shape and preset

Use `route-point-to-point`, expressed here as one continuous drivable route from a city start to a separate snowy finish. The closest preset is Sprint / Drag, retaining its point-to-point shape and core start, corridor, and finish treatment while extending it through the three requested environments.

## Config requirements

Draw `startpoint-line` as a clear launch line on the city pavement, `lane-corridor` as consistently legible road edges throughout the changing surfaces, and `trigger-finish` as a visible finish gate in the snow area. The three environments are scene-specific geometry: a built-up paved city, loose desert sand, and a snow-covered icy region, with transitions that preserve one unbroken road.

## Layout requirements

Place the functional half of `startpoint-line` once at the city origin and the functional `trigger-finish` once across the exact terminal end in the snow region. Add `checkpoint-respawn` recovery points at the city-to-desert and desert-to-snow transitions, oriented forward on the route, so wrecked vehicles return to a valid surface. The finish trigger and two recovery points are invisible placement work; the visible start and finish forms belong in both stages.

## Layout components

Build three sequential zones: city first, desert second, and snow third. Connect them with one non-branching road whose material changes from pavement to loose sand to icy road. Keep the city start and snowy endpoint spatially separate, use roadside scenery to make each transition unmistakable, and enclose the route with readable road boundaries rather than disconnected barriers. Frame the full A-to-B progression so all three environments read in order.

## Render order

Use `topdown`: render the top-down plan first and treat it as authoritative, then dress the isometric view from it, because a broken or ambiguous A-to-B route would make the race unplayable.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost

Use a large scale, driven by three distinct environment zones along one course. The theme is an all-climate road race from urban pavement through desert sand to frozen roads. Route through `P6` because Racing requires the continuous track to be valid by construction.
