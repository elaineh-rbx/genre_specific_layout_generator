## Enriched image prompt
Create a clean sci-fi floating combat arena formed by a compact 5-by-5 grid of 25 modular platforms above an abyss. Each tile is about 40 player-widths across and holds cover or a small enterable building. Show mechanical seams, control terminals, rails, actuators, and visibly movable infrastructure. Compose the grid during a shift: some platforms remain level, some rotate, some rise into high-ground positions, and some descend toward a lower hazard layer, while displaced cover and buildings remain attached to their tiles.

## Genre
Shooter is dominant. This is a last-squad-standing battle-royale concept in which ranged cover and high ground are manipulated.

## Shape and preset
Use `arena-stacked`, CombatZone (Multi-Level Arena), from outside Shooter's usual set because raised and lowered tiles create playable surfaces above one another. The preset is none; the physical rearranging grid replaces the defining layout bundle of ordinary Battle Royale.

## Config requirements
Use `tile-grid` from Party & Casual for the 25 identifiable modular tiles; `obstacle-moving` from Obby & Platformer for rotating, rising, and dropping platforms; `cover-los` for movable cover; `cover-elevated` for raised firing positions; `hazard-kill` for the abyss and lower hazard zone; `button-solve` from Puzzle for visible tile-shift control terminals; and `building-interior` for small buildings that move with their tiles.

## Layout requirements
For `button-solve`, place a Button interaction trigger at each authored terminal, linked to an eligible tile shift. Tile modules require explicit state anchors for level, rotated, raised, and lowered positions; their interfaces must remain mechanically compatible after movement.

## Layout components
The map contains exactly 25 square combat tiles in five rows and five columns. Mechanical seams divide them, with open abyss at the perimeter and lower hazard space beneath. Cover, terminals, and occasional small buildings stay inside tile footprints. Lift structures and rotation joints make each tile's possible movement readable in frame.

## Render order
Use `topdown`: establish the authoritative 5-by-5 adjacency and safe tile interfaces first, then dress the plan isometrically; a valid modular grid is essential but no supported authored maze or race blueprint applies.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
Use large scale because 25 tiles at roughly 40 player-widths each must share one frame. The theme is clean modular science fiction. Route `P2` for stacked tile states, `P3` for enterable buildings moving between outside and inside play, and `P6` because tile interfaces and rearranged adjacencies must remain valid by construction.
