# Scene prompt
Create a 3D racing game map with a race track suitable for a computer-controlled opponent.

# Agent decision
## Genre
The genre is Racing. The player races a computer-controlled opponent on a finite track.

## Shape and preset
Use `route-circuit`, a closed loop that both racers can follow repeatedly without ambiguous crossings. The matching preset is Circuit Racing. Because the brief is thin, assume one compact conventional circuit rather than inventing multiple environments or stunt geometry.

## Config requirements
Use `lane-corridor` for clearly painted track edges that keep the racing route readable to both player and computer opponent. Use `barrier-guardrail` around the outside of turns and `path-turnaround` for the circuit’s broad bends. Draw `spawn-grid` as two aligned starting slots and draw `startpoint-line` across the track.

## Layout requirements
Place the functional halves of `spawn-grid` and `startpoint-line` at one straight segment, with two slots: one player and one computer opponent. Place `trigger-lap` across that same start/finish straight to count completed laps. Add `checkpoint-respawn` recovery points after the start and at major track sectors, aligned to the route direction. The lap count is unspecified and remains a gameplay choice.

## Layout components
Build one compact closed racing circuit on a single level, with a start/finish straight, several broad turns, clear track-edge markings, and guardrails. Keep the route wide enough for two racers to pass and eliminate branches or self-crossings so computer navigation is straightforward. Use a simple open infield and a bounded outer verge. Compose the whole loop legibly in plan.

## Render order
Use `authored_plan`: a racing circuit is one of the supported computed blueprints, and the loop must close correctly before dressing.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
Scale is medium, sufficient for a readable two-racer circuit. No visual theme was specified, so use a neutral bright arcade-racing treatment. Use `P6` because Racing and the closed circuit require valid-by-construction topology.
