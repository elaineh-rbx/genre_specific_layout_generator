# Agent decision
## Clarifications resolved
- [author] shape — Q: Do players see the battlefield from above and place units, or run around as soldiers? A: Players run around as soldiers in third-person, building and defending their own bases while attacking others.
- [author] scale — Q: How many bases share a map, and how far apart should they sit? A: Use a small map with 4 player bases, placed far enough apart that each base has its own defensible area but can be reached on foot without a long journey.

## Enriched image prompt
Create a small third-person World War II-inspired battlefield with exactly four player base territories distributed evenly around open contested ground. Each base has its own defensible footprint and broad adjacent land for player-built structures, while short walkable approaches connect every territory through the centre without making travel lengthy. Screen each base arrival area from direct enemy sightlines and provide substantial battlefield cover between bases. Include one custom hostile developer tank named “Siger & nex,” with an unobstructed entry and roaming route that can reach the central ground and threaten all four bases. Keep the four territories equally readable in one isometric frame and avoid fixed lanes; soldiers attack and defend dynamically across the shared terrain.

## Genre
The dominant genre is Strategy, because building, defending, and attacking territorial bases is the organizing loop. Action is secondary because players physically run and fight as third-person soldiers rather than commanding from above.

## Shape and preset
Use `terrain-open`, named Zone (Open Contested Terrain): four bases surround one shared battlefield with dynamic movement rather than a hard-coded lane. The matched preset is Real-Time Strategy, retaining its open terrain, territorial build land, and defendable base objectives while dropping unsupported resource sites.

## Config requirements
Use `buildzone-plot` for four broad territorial building areas radiating from the four starting bases. Use the visible half of `tracker-core` for a clear defendable heart in each base. Use `cover-los` for substantial walls, wreckage, and battlefield structures that break long sightlines between territories. Use the visible half of `spawn-protected` to screen each base arrival area from immediate cross-map attacks.

## Layout requirements
Place four `tracker-core` objectives, one at the protected heart of each base territory. Place four `spawn-protected` player spawn zones, one inside each base and behind its initial defensive geometry. Place one `spawner-npc` origin for the custom “Siger & nex” tank along the battlefield perimeter, connected to a broad roaming route that reaches the centre and all four base approaches.

## Layout components
The map has four equal base zones placed around one open central conflict zone. Each base contains a protected spawn edge, a central defendable objective, and buildable land extending outward without overlapping another territory. Short roads or open approaches connect each base to the centre and to neighboring territories; no route is a forced lane. Battlefield cover interrupts direct views but leaves the tank enough width to move. A continuous perimeter bounds the small map. Compose the four bases symmetrically around the midground centre so their separation, approach distances, and shared tank threat are visible together.

## Render order
Use `topdown`: establish the four balanced territories, short approach distances, central contested ground, and tank access first, then dress the isometric view. The top-down plan is authoritative because unfair spacing or a blocked base approach would undermine the attack-and-defense layout.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
The scale band is medium, despite the author's “small map” wording, because four individual base compounds and their defensive geometry must remain legible while staying within a short walk. The theme is a stylized World War II battlefield with player-built fortifications and a custom armored threat. `P0` is sufficient: all four bases and routes occupy one single-surface map without overhangs, interiors, separate maps, or structure-critical fixed lanes.
