# Scene prompt
Create a compact, walkable few-hundred-stud North Pole exploration map with snowy ground and ice formations, arranged as a searchable outdoor area. Place 10 penguins hidden throughout distinct spots across the map, with clear routes between the foreground, central area, and outer edges.

# Agent decision
## Genre
The dominant genre is Adventure: the game is a finite outdoor scavenger hunt for hidden penguins. Progressive hints are gameplay information and do not add another spatial genre.

## Shape and preset
Use `world-open`, one compact North Pole area that can be searched freely in any order. The preset is Scavenger Hunt, retaining its collectible targets and hidden discovery spaces while omitting a physical quest board because the requested hints need no map geometry.

## Config requirements
Use `alcove-secret` for small searchable recesses among snowbanks and ice formations where penguins can be concealed without becoming unreachable. Use `path-trail` for clear walkable routes linking the foreground, central area, and outer edges. Keep the snowy ground as one continuous outdoor surface with freestanding ice formations rather than caves or overhangs.

## Layout requirements
Place exactly 10 `collectible-nodes` as hidden penguins, each in a distinct reachable spot. Distribute them across the foreground, central area, and outer edge band, with enough separation that no single viewpoint reveals several at once. Align every hiding place with a walkable route or short searchable spur, and keep the penguins clear of collision-heavy ice props.

## Layout components
Build one compact, few-hundred-stud snowy search zone. Organize it into a readable foreground arrival area, a denser central field of stylized ice formations, and a broad outer edge ring. Connect these regions with a main loop and short branch paths, preserving clear return routes and avoiding dead-end traps. Use snowbanks, ice pillars, low ridges, and scattered frozen props to create ten distinct hiding contexts while keeping all play on the same ground layer. Bound the map with taller ice formations and distant polar scenery. Compose the overview with the central field as the anchor and routes visibly fanning toward every edge.

## Render order
Use `isometric`: establish the snowy North Pole look and hiding geometry first, then derive the top-down plan, because this search space does not require computed maze or circuit topology.

## Scale, theme, and pipeline cost
The scale is medium, driven by the explicitly walkable few-hundred-stud footprint and 10 separated finds. The theme is bright, readable North Pole snow and ice. Use `P0`; the scene is one connected outdoor surface with no overhangs, interiors, separate maps, or structural-generation constraint.
